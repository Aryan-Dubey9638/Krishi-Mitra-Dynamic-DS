# Quality Standards & Grading Contracts
## Agricultural Commodity Export & Domestic Trade Specifications

---

## Part 1: Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Grading

The BIS and AGMARK certification bodies define mandatory grading standards for
agricultural produce sold in interstate commerce and exports.

### AGMARK Certification Process:
1. Farmer submits samples to state AGMARK grading laboratory.
2. Moisture, size, color, and defect testing (48-hour turnaround).
3. AGMARK stamp applied to certified bags.
4. Certified produce commands 5–15% premium in wholesale markets.
5. Mandatory for export consignments > 1 metric tonne.

## Part 2: Export Quality Specifications

### Tomato — Export Grade (EU/Middle East Markets)
- Minimum diameter: 57mm (Class I), 47mm (Class II)
- Color uniformity: ≥ 90% surface red/pink
- Total Soluble Solids (TSS): 4.5–5.5°Brix
- pH: 4.0–4.5
- Pesticide residue: Within EU MRL (Maximum Residue Limits)
- Packaging: 10–12 kg cartons, single layer, cushioned
- Cold chain: 8–10°C, 90–95% relative humidity
- Shelf life at destination: Minimum 7 days

FOB price (Mumbai): ₹35–₹55 per kg (Grade I)
Air freight: ₹85–₹120 per kg additional for Gulf/Europe

### Onion — Export Grade (Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, UK)
- Bulb size: 60–80mm (preferred), 45–60mm (acceptable)
- Moisture: < 13% (by weight)
- Pungency: > 20 pyruvic acid units (μmol/g FW) for Gulf markets
- Export packaging: 10 kg jute/PP mesh bags
- Nashik (Maharashtra): Largest export hub, ₹2,500–₹4,500/q FOB
- Egypt competes heavily; Indian onion dominates Sri Lanka, Malaysia

### Potato — Export Grade
- Size: 50–80mm, oval/round
- Specific gravity: > 1.07 (for chips/crisps processing)
- Reducing sugars: < 0.25% (processing industry requirement)
- Main export destinations: Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka
- Cold storage capability (2–4°C) required for export-quality maintenance

## Part 3: Contract Farming Specifications

### Model Contract Terms (Common in Punjab/Maharashtra):

**Clause 1 — Quality Commitment:**
The farmer agrees to supply Grade A produce as defined in Schedule I (attached).
A price deduction of 15% shall apply to any Grade B lot delivered against a Grade A contract.
Grade C lots shall be rejected at the company's discretion.

**Clause 2 — Moisture Limits:**
All cereals (Wheat, Maize, Soybean) to be delivered at ≤ 12% moisture.
Price deduction of ₹25 per quintal per 1% excess moisture beyond limit.

**Clause 3 — Price Linkage:**
Procurement price = Modal price at Azadpur Mandi (Delhi) on delivery date
                   × (1 - discount_factor)
Where discount_factor = 0.05 for Grade A; 0.10 for Grade B.

**Clause 4 — Transport Responsibility:**
For contracts > 100 quintals: Company provides dedicated transport from farm gate.
For contracts < 100 quintals: Farmer delivers to designated aggregation center.
Transport cost recovery: ₹0.80 per quintal per km charged to farmer for distances > 50 km.

**Clause 5 — Payment Terms:**
- 80% advance on weight receipt at mandi
- 20% balance within 7 working days post quality certification
- NEFT/RTGS payments only; no cash transactions above ₹2,000

## Part 5: Dispute Resolution

Under APMC Act Section 37, disputes between farmers and commission agents are resolved by:
1. Market Committee mediation (within 15 days)
2. APMC Arbitrator hearing (within 30 days)
3. State Agricultural Marketing Board appellate authority (within 60 days)
4. Civil court as last resort

Common disputes involve: weighment discrepancies, quality rejection without testing,
payment delays beyond 48 hours, and unauthorized deductions from sale proceeds.
