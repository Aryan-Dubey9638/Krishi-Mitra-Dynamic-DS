# APMC Act — Agricultural Produce Market Committee Regulations
## Compiled Reference Document for Topo-RAG Narrative Store

---

## 1. Introduction to APMC Acts

The Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) Act governs the buying and selling of
agricultural produce in India. Each state has its own APMC Act with specific provisions.
The central purpose is to ensure farmers receive fair prices for their produce by regulating
market practices at designated mandi (wholesale market) locations.

The APMC system establishes licensed trading zones where all transactions of notified
agricultural commodities must be conducted. Market fees and commissions are legally capped
under these acts to prevent exploitation of farmers.

---

## 2. Market Fees and Commission Structure

Under standard APMC regulations:

- **Market Fee (Mandi Tax):** Ranges from 0.5% to 2.0% of the value of produce traded.
  Most states charge between 1.0% and 1.5%.
- **Commission Agent Fee (Arhatiyas):** Licensed commission agents charge 2% to 4% of
  the transaction value. Maharashtra caps it at 2.0%; Punjab at 2.5%; UP at 3.0%.
- **Loading/Unloading Charges:** Standardized at ₹10–₹20 per quintal depending on
  the commodity and market.
- **Weighment Charges:** ₹2–₹5 per quintal.

Maharashtra APMC Amendment 2016 removed licensing requirements for direct farmer-to-buyer
transactions, reducing intermediary costs significantly for commodities like Tomato, Onion,
and Potato in certain designated markets.

## 3. Quality Grading Standards

### Tomato (Lycopersicum esculentum)

**Grade A (Premium)**:
- Diameter: > 55mm
- Color: Uniform red/pink, no green patches
- Firmness: Firm to touch, no soft spots
- Blemishes: Zero tolerance for cracks or pest damage
- Moisture: < 93%
- Shelf life: 7–10 days post-harvest under ambient conditions
- Price premium over Grade B: 12–18%

**Grade B (Standard)**:
- Diameter: 40–55mm
- Color: Mostly uniform, minor green patches permissible
- Firmness: Slight give acceptable
- Blemishes: < 5% surface area affected
- Moisture: < 95%
- Shelf life: 4–7 days

**Grade C (Processing/Industrial)**:
- Below Grade B specifications
- Used for ketchup, sauce, and paste manufacturing
- Price: 25–35% below Grade B modal price

### Onion (Allium cepa)

**Grade A:**
- Bulb diameter: > 60mm
- Dry outer skin: ≥ 3 layers, golden/red
- Neck diameter: < 1.5cm (well-cured)
- No sprouting, no mechanical damage
- Price premium: 10–15% over Grade B

**Grade B:**
- Bulb diameter: 45–60mm
- Minor skin defects permissible
- Maximum 3% moisture content by weight

**Grade C:**
- < 45mm diameter, soft bulbs, sprouting permissible
- Export or processing use only

### Potato (Solanum tuberosum)

**Grade A:**
- Size: > 50mm, uniform round/oval
- No greening, sprouts < 3mm
- No hollow heart, no black scurf
- Price premium: 8–10% over Grade B

**Grade B:**
- Size: 35–50mm
- Minor surface defects < 5% area
- Up to 5% foreign matter by weight

## 4. Wastage Norms and Post-Harvest Losses

India experiences one of the highest post-harvest loss rates globally. Standard wastage
norms adopted by APMC and logistics providers:

| Commodity  | Farm Gate Loss | Transit Loss | Mandi Handling Loss | Total Typical Loss |
|------------|---------------|--------------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Tomato     | 5–8%          | 4–7%         | 2–4%                | 11–19%             |
| Onion      | 2–4%          | 2–3%         | 1–2%                | 5–9%               |
| Potato     | 2–3%          | 1–2%         | 1–2%                | 4–7%               |
| Wheat      | 0.5–1%        | 0.5–1%       | 0.5%                | 1.5–2.5%           |
| Rice       | 1–2%          | 0.5–1%       | 0.5%                | 2–3.5%             |
| Garlic     | 4–6%          | 3–5%         | 1–2%                | 8–13%              |

Cold chain storage (0–4°C for Tomato; 0–2°C for Potato) can reduce total losses to 3–5%.

## 5. Transport Regulations

### Lorry/Truck Requirements:
- Vehicles carrying perishables must be covered/tarpaulin-protected.
- Refrigerated transport (reefer trucks) mandatory for long-haul (>600km) for Tomato and Leafy Vegetables.
- Maximum load per truck: 25 metric tonnes for standard SH vehicles; 40 MT for NH multi-axle.

### State-level checkpost charges:
- **Maharashtra:** ₹50–₹100 per truck per checkpost
- **UP:** ₹75–₹150 per truck
- **Rajasthan:** ₹40–₹80 per truck
- **Punjab/Haryana:** ₹30–₹60 per truck

### National Highway Transport Tariffs (2024):
- Produce in 5–7 tonne load: ₹0.75–₹0.95 per quintal per km
- Produce in 15–20 tonne load: ₹0.60–₹0.80 per quintal per km
- Reefer transport surcharge: +35–50% above standard tariff

## 6. Price Discovery Mechanism at Mandis

Most Indian mandis use one of three price discovery systems:

1. **Open Auction (Haraaj/Lelang):** Commission agent announces opening price; buyers bid upward.
   Most common for vegetables and fruits. Transparent but subject to cartel behavior.

2. **Closed Tender:** Written bids submitted in sealed envelopes; highest bidder wins.
   Used primarily for large agricultural lots (>10 tonnes) and government procurement.

3. **Electronic Trading (e-NAM):** National Agricultural Market platform launched 2016.
   Now covers 1,000+ mandis across India. Enables inter-state trade, online payment,
   and assay-based (quality-tested) price discovery. Reduces commission to 0.5–1.0%.

e-NAM integration significantly reduces price spread between mandis in the same region.
A farmer in Nashik can now access Pune or Mumbai modal prices without physical transport.

## 7. Seasonal Price Patterns

Commodity prices at Indian mandis exhibit strong seasonal variation:

**Tomato:**
- Peak supply (low prices): October–December (Kharif harvest), March–May (Rabi harvest)
- Peak prices: July–September (monsoon disruption), January–February (post-Rabi gap)
- Typical price range: ₹500/q (flush season) to ₹6,000/q (off-season)

**Onion:**
- Flush season (low prices): November–January (Kharif), May–June (Rabi)
- Scarcity (high prices): August–October, February–April
- Major production: Nashik, Solapur (Maharashtra) account for 40% of national supply

**Potato:**
- Harvest (low prices): February–March (UP, Punjab main harvest)
- Storage arbitrage: Cold storage in Agra and Jalandhar holds potatoes for 6–8 months;
  prices peak in October–November (+₹400–700/q over harvest price)

## 8. Farmer Advisory Summary

1. **Target Grade A production** to earn 10–20% premium; invest in sorting/grading machines.
2. **Use e-NAM** for price comparison before committing to local mandi auction.
3. **Optimize transport timing:** Avoid peak monsoon months for perishables.
4. **Cold chain investment:** ₹2–₹4 per kg cold storage cost can save 10–15% wastage loss.
5. **APMC reform awareness:** Several states (Maharashtra, Telangana, Karnataka) now allow
   direct sale to retailers/processors without mandi route — check local exemptions.
